The Ideology Research Group, based at the University of Edinburgh but welcoming international members, aims at
discussing ideology broadly construed, with informal meetings, both in-person and online, where members present
work or discuss literature.

## Our research goals

We aim to work towards answering the following questions:

* **What is ideology?** We take an ideology to be a bundle of {norms, beliefs, attitudes, expectations, procedures,
    relationship patterns}, that serve to justify particular behaviours and social relationships, and that are socially
    transmitted and socially dynamic in that our preference for an ideology depends on others' preference for it. For
    example, misogyny is an ideology that sets out rights and duties between men and women — women have the duty to give
    men sexual attention and emotional care; men owe women money or purported chivalry. Women who deviate from these
    norms are punished through enforcement procedures, such as humiliation.
* **When is ideology harmful?** An ideology could be harmful because of its social effects, eg the ideology of
    fatphobia. Or an ideology could be harmful for epistemic reasons. For example, wage labour is an ideology — but it's
    also historically contigent. If I start to believe that production could not be organised in any other way than wage
    labour, this ideology becomes reified, rigidified, naturalised. This is harmful because it is epistemically
    limiting. When else is ideology harmful, and how can one evaluate harm independently of any one ideology?
* **How does ideology self-reproduce?** Successful ideologies are self-reproducing by way of a positive feedback loop —
    if my preference for an ideology depends on others' adherence to it, when many others adhere to it this might
    dispose me to adopt it as well, and my preference might cause others to adopt the ideology too, and so on. Through
    the reproduction of these ideologies, kinds of relationships are also reproduced: (capitalist) relationships of
    production, (sexist) personal relationships etc. Others' perceived adherence is also portrayed through resources
    that teach and reinforce eg misogynstic rules, like portrayals in media of women who comply with sexist rules as
    “good” and women who do not as “bad”. Related topics include socialisation, subjectification and governmentality.
* **What role does language play in transmission?** Normative generics are claims that promote
    essentialist-normative-enforcing attitudes. “Boys don't cry” is not a mere statistical observation; it implies a
    belief about the essence of being a boy (not crying), a normative expectation that this essence should be upheld,
    and enforcement methods that dictate what should be done about norm violations (“boys who cry should be laughed
    at”). Similarly: “fat people are disgusting” and “a woman puts her family before her career”; but also “shareholders
    exploit employees”, “health insurance companies do violence”.
* **What are possible reasons to adhere to an ideology?** For example: negative sanctions, ie a desire to avoid
    punishment; positive sanctions, ie a desire to please; epistemic deference, ie a presumption that the ideology is
    justified; self-image or roles, ie a perception of oneself as someone who would engage in actions and relationships
    the ideology prescribes. What else?
* **How can harmful ideology be changed?** Sally Haslanger says that, to achieve social justice, it is not enough to
    change laws or individual attitudes — we must also change culture, ie change ideologies. She suggests doing this
    through “social movements and contentious politics”. How can an ideology's self-reproduction be halted or reversed?
    Also, how does ideology interact with laws? Cristina Bicchieri says that “when laws exist but are in conflict with
    social norms, social norms win”.
* **How can we best study ideology?** The literature on ideology is multifaceted, elusive, and carries a lot of baggage.
    Ideology means different things to different thinkers. Some evaluate ideology neutrally, negatively or positively.
    What contributions to understanding ideology can be made by different traditions, such as social norms, game theory,
    analytic philosophy, Foucauldian discourse analysis, Sewellian schemas+resources, critical theory,
    post-structuralism, score-keeping in language games, Marxism and post-Marxism? Are positivism and scientific realism
    suitable lenses through which to study ideology?
* **…and other questions.** Is ideology a shared mental or social representation? Can it be reduced to individual
    attitudes, or is it something above and beyond individuals? Does ideology cause alienation? When are we complicit in
    upholding ideology? How much power do we have to shape ideology and how much power does it have to shape us and our
    social environment? (How) can we judge the epistemic and normative value of ideological positions? Are “ideology”
    and “culture” the same thing? Does ideology need to be internally coherent?
